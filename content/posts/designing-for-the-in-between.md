The most interesting work rarely sits neatly inside one discipline. It lives in the in-between: where a brand promise meets a product detail, where a technical constraint becomes a creative prompt, where the thing we make meets the life someone is already living.

## Start with the seams

I used to think good collaboration meant smoothing everything over. Align early, make the handoff clean, keep the process frictionless. But a little friction can be useful. It tells you where two perspectives haven’t met yet.

That moment when a designer asks *“what if it felt more like this?”* and an engineer replies *“we could make that happen if…”* is where the work starts to become its own thing.

## Leave room for the unexpected

The best outcomes I’ve been part of weren’t the ones where everyone followed the plan perfectly. They were the ones where people stayed curious enough to change the plan when the work asked for it.

So I’m trying to leave more space between the lines. Less rushing towards the neat answer. More attention to what’s already emerging.

*Make the space. See what shows up.*
